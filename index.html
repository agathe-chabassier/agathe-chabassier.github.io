About

I am a PhD candidate designing and applying causal inference methods to generate robust real-world evidence (RWE) from connected-device data – long, dense series of measurements, irregularly spaced and recorded by users themselves over years. My PhD is supervised by Julie Josse, Erwan Scornet and Pierre Binon, with Sorbonne Université (LPSM), Inria (PreMeDICaL team) and Withings.

My research estimates the causal effect of an intervention on biomarker trajectories over time – capturing real-life dynamics such as rebound or yo-yo effects. My latest methodological work, Shape-preserving treatment effects for functional outcomes, is available on arXiv. At Withings, I also supervise applications of these methods to wearable data, medical claims and questionnaires.

My RWE work began at Institut Curie, where I performed causal inference analyses on the French National Health Data System (SNDS) and clinical questionnaire data, applying target trial emulation, survival analysis and propensity score methods.

Trained in mathematics, statistics and machine learning (MVA programme at ENS Paris-Saclay and engineering degree from Mines Paris – PSL), I have applied data science across medical imaging, genomics, epidemiology and computational neuroscience.

Why trajectory shape matters

individual patients elastic average pointwise average

When treated patients follow the same response pattern, such as weight loss followed by a rebound, each on their own timeline, averaging trajectories point by point blurs this common pattern. Elastic causal methods capture the typical shape and timing of the treatment effect.
